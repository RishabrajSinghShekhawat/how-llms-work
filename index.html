Chapter 01

The Fundamental Mental Model

Before touching mathematics or matrices, you must understand what an LLM actually is—and what it isn't. Many people mistakenly think an LLM is a database of facts or a sentient brain. In reality, it is something far more elegant: The world's most sophisticated pattern completion system.

✕

The Misconception: A "Thinker" or "Search Engine"

People assume the AI browses an internal encyclopedia, verifies logical truth, thinks about its opinions, and outputs retrieved facts.

  • • It does not store paragraphs of text like a hard drive.
  • • It has no concept of "objective truth"—only statistical correlations in language.
  • • It cannot pause to think unless prompted to generate step-by-step tokens.
Result: Confusing hallucinations with intentional lies.
✓

The Reality: Autocomplete on Trillions of Dimensions

An LLM is a giant mathematical function. Given a sequence of preceding words, its entire objective is to answer one question:

"Based on everything human beings have ever written, what single piece of text most likely comes next?"

Because human language contains deep reasoning, logic, code, sentiment, and nuance, predicting the next word with extreme accuracy requires the model to simulate reasoning itself.

Insight: Intelligence emerges from ultra-high-dimensional compression.

The End-to-End LLM Lifecycle at a Glance

Every query you send to ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini travels through these exact 4 stages:

01

Tokenization

Text is chopped into sub-word chunks and converted into numeric IDs (vocabulary lookup).

02

Embedding Space

Each number turns into a coordinate vector in thousands of mathematical dimensions that capture meaning.

03

Transformer Layers

04

Probabilities & Sampling

The model outputs odds for every word in its dictionary, picks one, appends it, and loops again!

Chapter 02

Tokenization: Computers Cannot Read Letters

Computers only understand numbers. If you feed the word "artificial" into a neural network, it can do nothing with the ASCII letters. The model must first translate your text into Tokens via algorithms like Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE).

Why not just whole words or single letters?

If we tokenize character-by-character, sentences become 10x longer, requiring massive computing power. If we tokenize whole words, our vocabulary list would need millions of words and would crash when encountering typos or new slang. Subwords (BPE) give the perfect balance—rare words are split into known syllables (e.g. "Anti-dis-establish-ment"), while common words stay compact.

Chapter 03

Vector Embeddings: Mapping Meaning into Space

Having a token ID like 4920 is useless because the number itself has no meaning (is 4920 close to 4921? No). To give words meaning, every token is transformed into an Embedding Vector—a list of thousands of floating-point numbers representing coordinates in a multi-dimensional semantic universe.

Semantic 2D Coordinate Map

Words with similar contexts naturally clump together in embedding space.

Vector Relation: King - Man + Woman ≈ Queen
Drag nodes or click to compute semantic distance Real LLMs use 4,096 to 12,288 dimensions!

What Do The Dimensions Mean?

Imagine every coordinate represents an abstract linguistic attribute:

Dimension 1: [Royalty] King (+0.95), Apple (-0.88)
Dimension 2: [Gender/Femininity] Queen (+0.92), King (-0.90)
Dimension 3: [Edible Fruit] Apple (+0.99), Banana (+0.98)

No human labels these dimensions! The model self-discovers these relationships through gradient descent during pre-training.

Cosine Similarity (Measuring Meaning)

To check if two concepts are related, the model measures the angle $\theta$ between their vectors:

$$\cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\|}$$

$\cos = 1.0$ means identical meaning. $\cos = 0$ means unrelated. $\cos = -1$ means opposite concepts.

Chapter 04

The Secret Weapon: Self-Attention Mechanism

Static embeddings have a catastrophic flaw: the word "bank" in "river bank" and "bank account" would share the exact same vector! In 2017, Google researchers published "Attention Is All You Need", introducing Self-Attention: a mechanism that allows words to look at every other word in a sentence and update their meaning dynamically based on context.

Key Insight

Resolving Ambiguity with Context

When the model reads the token "it", self-attention directs 78% of its attention weight back to "animal" and only 4% to "street", because the adjective "tired" semantically associates with biological creatures, not paved roads!

The Triad: Queries, Keys & Values

Think of YouTube or a database search:

  • Query (Q): What am I looking for? (e.g. "Who is tired?")
  • Key (K): What label do I have? (e.g. "I am an animal")
  • Value (V): What content do I hold? (The actual feature vector)
$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V$$
Chapter 05

Next-Token Prediction & The Art of Sampling

Once the Transformer processes the entire prompt through 32 to 128 layers of attention, it produces raw numbers called Logits for all 50,000+ words in its vocabulary. Converting these logits into probabilities via Softmax is how the model chooses what to print next.

The astronaut looked through the spaceship window and saw the [NEXT_TOKEN?]
Temperature: 0.70 Balanced & Fluent
0.1 (Strict/Code) 0.7 (Default) 1.5 (Wild/Creative)

What does temperature do? It divides the logits: $e^{z_i / T}$. At low $T$, high probabilities get exaggerated (deterministic). At high $T$, all words become almost equally probable (unhinged chaos).

Chapter 06

The Making of a Model: 3 Stages of Training

A raw neural network knows nothing. How do we turn billions of randomly initialized weights into a helpful assistant like ChatGPT or Gemini? Modern AI follows an industry-standard 3-step pipeline.

Stage 01 99% of Computing Cost

Pre-Training (Raw Text World Model)

The model reads thousands of gigabytes of text: Wikipedia, books, GitHub code, scientific papers, and high-quality web pages.

• Hardware: 10,000+ GPUs cluster (H100 / TPU)
• Duration: 3 - 6 months continuously
• Cost: $50M - $200M+ per training run
How a Base Model Behaves:
User prompt:
"Write a poem about the ocean."
Base Model Output (Merely completes pattern):
"Write a poem about the sky. Write a poem about the trees. Write a poem about the mountains..."

A base model doesn't know it is supposed to be helpful! It just thinks this is a list of homework exercises.

Stage 02 Curated Quality

Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT / Instruction)

Expert human annotators write hundreds of thousands of ideal dialogues: high-quality questions paired with thorough, polite, accurate responses.

• Goal: Transform text completer into an assistant
• Formats: Dialogues, System prompts, Roleplay
• Scale: 100k - 1M highly curated conversations
How an SFT Model Behaves:
User prompt:
"Write a poem about the ocean."
SFT Model Output (Follows Instructions):
"Rolling waves of emerald green,
The deepest depths no eyes have seen.
Tides that whisper to the shore..."

The model now understands the conversational contract: "When asked a question, provide the answer."

Stage 03 Safety & Polish

Alignment: RLHF & DPO

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). The model generates multiple candidate answers; humans or judge models rank: "Response A is better and safer than Response B".

• Reward Model learns what humans prefer
• Suppresses dangerous advice, bias, and toxic slurs
• Teaches concise formatting and admitted uncertainty
Human Preference Ranking:
Option A (Accepted ✓)
Accurate, cited, structured, helpful formatting.
Option B (Rejected ✕)
Hallucinates non-existent facts, verbose, condescending.

A mathematical penalty prevents the model from straying too far from its original knowledge while maximizing human rating.

Chapter 07

Crucial Concepts: Context, Memory & Hallucinations

To use and build with LLMs like an elite developer, you must know their architectural limits and runtime dynamics.

Context Window (Working Memory)

The context window (e.g. 128k - 1M tokens) is like the model's RAM. It holds your prompt and previous messages. Once text falls outside this window, the model has literally zero recollection of it.

Note: Weights are permanent ROM; Prompt is temporary RAM.

Why Do Models Hallucinate?

Because the model optimizes for plausibility, not factuality. If a fake scientific paper title sounds phonetically and grammatically like a real paper, the model will invent authors and citations without flinching.

Fix: RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation) & Tool Calling.

Chain-of-Thought (Scratchpad)

An LLM only computes a fixed number of FLOPs per token. If you ask a complex riddle and demand an immediate answer in 1 word, it fails. Asking it to "think step by step" gives it tokens to compute intermediate thoughts!

More output tokens = More FLOPs of reasoning.
